Greetings! I am Jhotheeshwar, currently a 3rd-year B.E. Electronics Engineering (VLSI Design and Technology) undergraduate at Chennai Institute of Technology (CIT Chennai). I completed all my schooling from 1st through 12th standard at Kendriya Vidyalaya.

My journey began around the age of 12. While most kids my age simply consumed technology, I couldn't stop questioning everything about how it actually functioned. How does a television beam moving pictures across a flat piece of glass? How can a simple pocket calculator solve complex arithmetic sums instantaneously? But the single question that captivated my mind and set my life's direction was:

“How can mere electricity solve mathematical problems?”

In Class 9, I was first introduced to the concept of programming—the realization that human logic could be translated into machine instructions. By Class 11, I began learning Python, which deepened my fascination with modern computing abstractions and the digital world.

After completing Class 12, I wanted to understand the physical hardware beneath the code. My brother suggested I explore the specialized VLSI (Very Large Scale Integration) degree at CIT Chennai. Researching the semiconductor industry and microchip fabrication quickly showed me that silicon is the physical realm where physics, electricity, and logic converge. It was the direct answer to the question I asked when I was 12, and I knew immediately that this was where I wanted to dedicate my engineering career.

Since joining college, I have developed numerous hardware projects, prototyping on FPGAs such as the Lattice CertusPro-NX, ZedBoard (Zynq-7000), AMD Kria KV260, and Tang Nano-9K, as well as microcontrollers and MPUs including the Renesas RA8P1 & RA6E2, Raspberry Pi 4, and ESP32.

Above all, I live by the philosophy that the true purpose of life is to learn something new every single day. That relentless curiosity pushes me to explore novel hardware architectures, optimize real-time algorithms, and dive into emerging tech.

Outside of engineering and academics, I believe in staying well-rounded: I enjoy swimming, playing chess, and hanging out with friends.

FAQs

Common questions regarding Edge AI, FPGA architecture & embedded engineering

An Edge-AI accelerator (such as an FPGA CNN engine or dedicated NPU like the Arm Ethos-U55) executes neural network tensor computations directly on localized hardware rather than streaming data to remote clouds. This drastically minimizes latency, cuts power consumption, guarantees deterministic timing, and preserves data privacy by processing feeds on-device.

μT-Kernel 3.0 is a standardized real-time operating system from the TRON Project. In high-speed vision systems, tasks like camera DMA frame capture, NPU inference, and LCD rendering must run concurrently with strict deterministic deadlines. An RTOS provides prioritized preemption, semaphore synchronization, and predictable latency that bare-metal code cannot cleanly manage.

Software encryption consumes significant CPU cycles and suffers from variable execution time. Implementing an 8-bit Substitution-Permutation Network in Verilog RTL transforms the cipher rounds into pure combinational logic and registers, executing in constant clock cycles with zero host CPU load.

Yes. All edge projects — LATTEYES (Lattice CertusPro-NX FPGA), Vision-x (Renesas RA8P1), SafeLink (ZedBoard Zynq-7000), and PiAssistant (offline Whisper + Gemma LLM) — are engineered to run completely autonomous, air-gapped, and independent of internet connectivity or cloud infrastructure.
